# ATLAS-01

## Workflow identity

> ATLAS-01: roughly two-metre-long unmanned American aerospace-inspired Mars rover. Low rectangular warm-white equipment body, navy-blue inset side panels, flat charcoal deck. Six broad open-metal-mesh wheels, three per side, connect visibly to the chassis through dark articulated suspension and clear hubs. One short central mast carries a compact horizontal pair of camera lenses. One short two-joint sampling arm mounts at the FRONT centre of the chassis, ending in a small cylindrical contact tool; it folds against the front for travel. The rear is a plain sealed service panel. Matte ceramic-painted panels, brushed metal arm sections, dark protected pivots, a few recessed bolts. No loose cables, decorative fins or passenger compartment. Keep wheel, axle and suspension orientations mechanically coherent.

## Scenes

### 01 — Hello world

First morning after landing. Front-left view of the entire ATLAS on a broad rust-coloured plain. A small landing platform and short ramp sit in the middle distance behind it, connected by two bands of fresh wheel tracks. Low sunlight from frame left catches mesh wheel edges and casts a long grounded shadow to the right. Freshly deployed body with only a scattering of grit on lower panels and wheel contact patches. Camera upright, sampling arm folded. Fine foreground pebbles and a low dark ridge on the horizon. Quiet operational survey photograph, not a promotional hero shot.

### 02 — Rock investigation

ATLAS examines a dark fractured basalt boulder beside its front wheel. Front-right photograph, close enough to read contact while keeping the whole rover in frame. The single FRONT arm unfolds through two connected joints, cylindrical tool pressed against one rough rock face. Wheels support the rover firmly, far wheels naturally occluded. A small fresh pale abrasion marks the contact, a few fallen grains immediately beneath it. Side sunlight reveals rock pits, mesh wheel texture and fine dust around suspension pivots. No extra arm, floating instrument or dramatic debris cloud.

[View image](scenes/02-rock-investigation.png)

### 03 — Three years later

The same ATLAS after three Earth years, paused beside a shallow eroded valley. Left-side view with front angled slightly toward camera, rover about half the frame width. Original white and navy panels remain recognizable under uneven ochre dust: thicker in seams and sheltered corners, thinner on exposed edges. Small lower-panel paint chips and rubbed wheel contact surfaces, no catastrophic damage. Arm folded, mast intact, all original equipment attached. A curved wheel trail crosses wind-rippled sediment behind it. Hazy late-afternoon light, layered valley wall in the distance. Worn but maintained, not abandoned.
